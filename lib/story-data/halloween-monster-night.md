# MonstersNOW Master Story

## Halloween Monster Night

**Format:** 8.5 × 8.5 inches, 32 interior pages  
**Audience:** Ages 3–8  
**Personalization tokens:** `{child_name}`, `{monster_name}`  
**Narrative:** 14 spreads / 28 pages  
**Target read-aloud length:** Approximately 900 words

## Master production rules

- `{monster_name}` is replaced by the transformed version of each customer's uploaded monster drawing.
- Never add, remove, or describe anatomical features, colors, clothing, accessories, size, texture, expression, or abilities for `{monster_name}`.
- Stage monster actions without assuming hands, feet, wings, facial features, speech, or a particular method of movement.
- Reserve a flexible clear area for the monster in every scene. Important props, faces, text, and clues must not overlap that area.
- `{child_name}` wears the same fall outfit throughout the story. The monster—not a costume worn by the child—is the personalized Halloween character.
- Depict `{child_name}` using the customer's selected boy or girl presentation in every illustration. No pronoun token or alternate story passage is required because the manuscript avoids gender-specific pronouns.
- Treat all personalized text areas as flexible templates. Test the longest supported child and monster names before print, and reduce type size or rebalance line breaks without changing the wording.
- Physical spreads follow standard bound-book pagination: even-numbered page on the left, odd-numbered page on the right.

**Personalized characters:** `{child_name}`, `{monster_name}`.

**Continuity:** Child begins with an empty treat bucket and the same fall outfit used throughout. Sky is dusky purple-orange. Monster scale must not be described by surrounding characters' reactions.

## Pages 8–9 — Spread 3: Whoosh!

**Final story text:**  
Just as the parade keeper reached for the star—

**WHOOOOSH!**

A gust of wind swept through the square. Hats went flying. Leaves blew everywhere. And the Monster Star rose straight off its cushion.

“The star!” someone shouted.

Over the roofs it went, leaving a trail of gold behind it.

The wind dropped. The cushion was empty. The giant lantern stayed dark.

Now what?

**Illustration direction:** A sweeping wind arc carries the star toward the neighborhood. Keep the action energetic but cheerful. The crowd reacts with surprise, never fear. Show a clear golden trail mixed with curled leaves.

**Personalized characters:** `{child_name}`, `{monster_name}`.

**Continuity:** The star travels toward the street already seen on pages 4–5. No character touches the star.

10. The parade banner remains repaired after pages 20–21.
